# README: the pleasures (pleasures.js)

For the lead to fold into README.md, beside the baths. Idea bullets first, then *Things to try* lines.

## Idea bullets

- **The hanging gardens**: comes of a village that knows concrete looking for
  room for another field and finding none, anywhere. So: up. Three terraces up a
  slope (or on brick piers, where there is no slope), each a little field six
  wide, five rows over the last and open to the sky all the way up, with a pool
  dug at the foot and a cistern at the top. The terraces are fields like
  any other, sown and reaped and raided by ducks, but they do not count against
  the fields a village keeps. Between the pool and the cistern, up under the
  terraces, runs an archimedes screw of iron, and the screw-turner stands at the
  crank and turns it, and sings, and every twenty strokes a cell of water comes
  up out of the pool and into the cistern. The cistern spills onto the top
  terrace, the top terrace onto the next, and the bottom one back into the pool:
  the same water, round and round, less what the wheat drinks on the way. The
  pool is topped up a bucket at a time
- **The steam organ**: comes of concrete, a show or two, and a furnace that has
  been worked hard. A copper tank of forty cells of water over a firebrick
  firebox, and on its lid a rack of seven tinned pipes from six to eighteen high,
  each with a valve where it leaves the lid. Before a show (and now and then on
  its own) the organist fires it, a coal at a time, until there is a head of
  steam under the lid; then opens the valves to a tune, one note at a time. The
  steam goes up whichever pipe is open, and every puff of it out of the top of a
  pipe is that pipe's note. A concert is about thirty notes. It plays at every
  fireworks show. The steam goes up to the top of the sky, cools, and comes back
  down as rain about where it rose: what lands in the gutter at the organ's foot
  the organist bails back into the tank, and the rest is rain on whatever is
  next to it. The audience sways. Somebody always says it is a bit loud. Shut
  every valve on a tank full of steam for long enough, and the organist opens
  one, to let it breathe
- **The duck palace**: comes of the scarecrow, forty raids on the wheat, and a
  valley with ducks in it, in whoever has shooed the most of them. If you can't
  beat them, house them: a plank deck at the edge of the biggest fresh water
  there is, half on the bank and half out over the water, a pavilion with a
  little dome on it (gold, if there is gold to spare; painted, if not), three
  nests, a ramp, and at the bank end a trough. Whoever reaps the wheat keeps a
  handful of seed back, and the feeder scatters it in the trough, and is mobbed.
  A duck will go a long way for seed in the trough, a good deal further than for
  the wheat, so the raids fall off. A duck at home on the deck has ducklings
  twice as often, and they hatch there. The eldest duck wears a crown. Throw a
  duck at it, and the ducks will not go near it for a while
- **The ice palace**: there is no weather in the valley, so this one is yours.
  Freeze the village (a snowman, and somebody skating, first), with snow lying
  about the houses, and they build a palace out of it, quick, before it thaws:
  packed-snow walls, a crenellated tower and a gatehouse, ice windows, an ice
  floor, and a lantern hanging from the skylight. Anybody free lends a hand,
  whatever else is going up. They skate in it, they throw snowballs round it,
  and they stand in it out of the cold. The lantern's warmth is real, and the
  skylight drips. When it warms up it melts, and once it is more water than
  palace it is a ruin, and they are sorry. The next time you freeze them, they
  build it again

## Things to try

- Paint stone over the tops of the steam organ's pipes, then HEAT the boiler
- COOL the steam organ in the middle of a tune
- Throw a duck at the duck palace
- Freeze the village with snow lying about. Then HEAT it
