# README: the great works that look up (heavens.js)

For the lead to merge into README.md's list of ideas (in the wonders section), and *Things to try*.

- **The observatory**: comes of glass, masonry, and a good many looks out from
  the watchtower (twenty, all told; it occurs to whoever has looked out most).
  A whitewashed dome on top of the tower, with a slit in its shoulder and a
  brass telescope through the slit (on the highest ground there is, if there is
  no tower). An astronomer goes up now and then and looks: at the moon, every
  time, and at the stars, which they join up into a constellation and name
  (THE BIG DUCK; THE LADLE; sometimes after somebody lately dead). It hangs over
  the valley, faintly, for a while. Every so often they say there will be
  falling stars tonight, and there are, and anybody with nothing better to do
  stands and watches. One shower in three, one of them comes down: a lump of iron
  with a nugget of gold in the middle of it, glowing, never anywhere near
  anybody's house. Somebody goes and stands by it until it is cool enough to
  pick up
- **The lighthouse**: comes of oil, glass, masonry, and a lake: a real one, a
  hundred and fifty cells of water at the least, which no valley has unless you
  give it one. It occurs to the first one to swim in it. A red and white tower on
  the bank, a ladder up the inside, a gallery with iron rails, and a glass
  lantern with two lamps and a gilded reflector. The keeper carries a barrel of
  oil up from the well's tank whenever it is getting low; a barrel is a minute
  or so of light. The beam goes round, and whoever it passes over thinks a
  little faster; a swimmer in trouble swims for it. Somebody out there sees it
  too, the first night, and comes to live in the valley. Let the oil run out and
  it goes dark, and everybody knows whose job that was
- **The foundry**: comes of iron tools, concrete, sixty bars of iron made, and
  whoever has worked the furnace most. A firebrick chamber in a firebrick stack,
  a tap at the foot either side, and a mould sunk under each: a trough for
  plates, and a cup for an engine bell. The founder tips it full of iron and
  coal in layers, works the bellows, and keeps the fire fed (raking the ash out
  as they go, which is more ash than the valley has ever had); when most of it
  is running, they knock the plug through and it runs into the mould like water,
  only brighter. Once it has set and cooled, it comes out as plates, a fifth of
  a bar each. It wants a great deal of coal. Only the moon rocket wants plates
- **The moon rocket**: comes of the moon looked at a dozen times, the foundry,
  concrete, a few flights on a rocket pack and a good many barrels pumped; the
  astronomer thinks of it. A concrete pad with a firebrick trench, a red girder
  gantry with a ladder up to a walkway, and a rocket: fifty-odd cast plates in a
  hollow shell, an engine bell under it, a porthole, red fins, a gilded nose. It
  wants twelve barrels of oil, carried a barrel at a time from the well. Then a
  crew of up to three (whoever has flown most on a rocket pack, the astronomer,
  and whoever has had the most ideas) climb the gantry and get in, the valley
  comes to watch from a safe way off, and there is a countdown. It goes up on a
  column of real fire, out of the top of the world, and a little while later
  there is a flag on the moon. The crew come back the way anybody comes back
  from space (over the pad, this time; the parachutes mostly open), with moon
  rock in their pockets, which they set out by the statue. They wear the helmets
  for good after that, and will tell you about it

## Things to try

- Watch the sky once the observatory is up. Then go and stand a peasant where
  the falling star is coming down
- Give them a lake (a big one), and some oil. Then strike the lantern with
  lightning while there is oil up there
- Knock a hole in the foundry's mould while it pours. Or pour water on it
- Set fire to something near the moon rocket once it is fuelled. Stand well
  back
